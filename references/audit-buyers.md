# Audit Playbook: Buyers

The "buyers" goal means the post should drive high-intent action toward a purchase, demo, DM, lead, or signup. The X algorithm doesn't directly score conversions, but the signals that correlate with high purchase intent are knowable.

## Primary Signals to Optimize For

Ordered by their alignment with buying intent:

1. **`profile_click`** — When a user clicks your profile after reading a post, they're qualifying you. This is the closest proxy to "interested enough to learn more."
2. **`follow_author`** — A follow is a multi-touch commitment, often a precursor to a DM or buy.
3. **`click`** — Clicks on the post itself (especially "show more" or a link). For posts with an offer, this is the conversion-adjacent signal.
4. **`share_via_dm`** — The single strongest organic-recommendation signal. When someone DMs your post to a friend, they're vouching for it. DM-shared posts get high audience-quality matches in OON discovery.
5. **`cont_click_dwell_time`** — Dwell time *after* the click. Critical: a buyer reads the linked content, doesn't just click and bounce.
6. **`reply`** — Sales-shaped replies (questions about pricing, fit, "is this for X?") signal high intent. Generic "great point!" replies do not.

## Signals to De-prioritize (counter-intuitively)

- **`favorite`** — Likes are cheap. A post optimized for likes (one-liners, dunks, hot takes) often *reduces* buyer signal because it pulls in low-intent audience.
- **`retweet`** — Same. Mass reposts dilute the signal that targets buyers.

You can have high likes and zero buyers. The goal is to *narrow* the engagement to high-intent action.

## What Buyer-Optimized Content Looks Like

### Pattern 1: Specific Pain → Specific Answer

A specific problem statement followed by a concrete frame for the solution. Optimized for `click` and `profile_click` — readers who recognize the pain click in to learn what the answer is.

Hook example: "If your podcast editor charges by the hour, here's how much they're overcharging you in 2026."

### Pattern 2: Counter-Conventional Wisdom + Evidence

A clean contradiction of a widely-held belief, backed with a number or anecdote that makes the contradiction unmissable. Drives `reply` (people argue), `quote` (people add their take), `profile_click` (they want to see who you are).

Hook example: "The 'post daily' advice is dead. I posted 4x last month and tripled my reach. Here's what changed."

### Pattern 3: The Offer-in-Disguise

A piece of free value that *is* a sales tool. The content stands alone, but the underlying machinery is clearly something you sell. Drives `share_via_dm` (people send to colleagues), `follow_author` (people want more of this), and `cont_click_dwell_time` (they read the whole thing).

Hook example: "I gave 12 podcast editors the same 60-min raw file. Here's what each one charged and how the deliverables compared."

### Pattern 4: The Specific Identifier

A post that names a specific audience so clearly that members of that audience self-identify. Drives `profile_click` and `follow_author` because the reader thinks: "This person gets me."

Hook example: "If you're a solo agency owner billing $5–15k/month, you're in the worst pricing tier. Here's why."

## What to Avoid for Buyers

- **Generic relatable content** — pulls likes but no commerce.
- **Comedy-only posts** — funny posts win likes, lose buyers.
- **Engagement bait** — drives the wrong type of audience.
- **Pure aspiration** — "Build the business you want!" creates no urgency. Specific pain creates urgency.
- **Hashtag stuffing** — increases muted-keyword risk without lift.

## Format Recommendations

- **Single posts > threads** for buyer signal. A tight, complete pitch in one post reads as confident. Threads risk losing readers between tweets.
- If using a thread: the first post must stand alone as a complete idea. The thread is bonus depth, not the whole pitch.
- Image attached: a chart, screenshot, or stat that visually demonstrates the pain or the answer. `photo_expand` is a positive signal AND increases dwell time.

## Audit Checklist for Buyers

For each piece of content to audit, check:

- [ ] Does the hook name a specific buyer-shaped pain or contradiction? (drives `click` and `profile_click`)
- [ ] Would a buyer DM this to a colleague who has the same pain? (drives `share_via_dm`)
- [ ] Does the post stand alone, or does it require following a thread? (single posts convert better)
- [ ] Is the CTA implicit or explicit? (implicit usually wins — "DM me for the playbook" reads as desperate; "the playbook is at [link]" reads as confident)
- [ ] Does it avoid like-bait phrasing? ("Save this 👇", "Bookmark this", "Share with someone who needs this")
- [ ] Is there a visual asset (image/chart/screenshot) for `photo_expand`?
- [ ] Profile + pinned post + bio aligned with this offer? (`profile_click` only converts if the profile delivers)

## Rewrite Heuristics

When a post scores poorly for buyers:

- **If the hook is generic** → replace with a specific pain statement.
- **If it's pulling for likes** → cut the joke or the dunk; replace with the specific frame.
- **If it has a hashtag** → remove it (mute risk + no lift).
- **If it's a thread** → collapse to one strong post; the rest becomes a linked piece.
- **If the CTA is loud** → make it quiet (or remove it; let the work do the selling).
